# The Yappie Pro

A free, open-source news and policy terminal for stories affecting Asian American, Native Hawaiian and Pacific Islander (AAPI) communities in the U.S. No ads, no tracking, no accounts, no server, no business model.

## What it does
- **Dashboard** (`/`, the first tab; the logo also leads here): a welcome page that gathers the rest of the site on one screen, built from the files the other pages already load (it adds no feed of its own). The page opens in the dashboard's layout straight away, with a grey loading state until the news arrives, so the news list never flashes up first. From the top: today's date with up to four highlights for the coming week (an election day, comment deadlines closing, congressional hearings, today's observance); **Featured coverage**, only stories from The Yappie (the newest four: a lead story with its picture and summary and three more, opening in the in-portal reader; stories shown here are left out of Top headlines); **Top headlines** (the news list without policy items, statements and research, with Breaking and Featured marks first); **Since your last visit** (new items per section, with *Mark all as seen*); **Coming up** (the next 7 days on the Calendar, at most two voting dates with a link to the rest); **Policy watch** (the latest policy items, then items open for comment with days left, then the ballot measures); **Latest statements**; **Number of the week** (one key figure, changing each Monday, with its source); **Issues**; and an **Organization spotlight** (changing each day, drawn from groups with recent items on the site). A box with nothing to show is left out. On a phone the boxes stack in order of importance. Typing in the search box on the dashboard opens the news list with that search.
- **News tab** (`/news`) from community outlets, general outlets (filtered to AAPI-relevant stories) and, optionally, Google News searches. **English-language items only.** Each item is a headline: clicking or tapping it opens a details pane (a full-screen sheet on phones) with an "Open source" button that goes to the original; headlines are not direct links. The page shows no summaries: excerpts are used only to tag stories (topic, community, state); the one exception is the subtitle on The Yappie's own stories.
- **The Yappie reader:** stories hosted on theyappie.com open in an in-portal article view. One click on desktop, one tap on a phone.
- **Issue topics:** Immigration, Civil Rights, Health, Elections, Economy, Housing, Education, Courts, Government, Pacific, Culture.
- **Community and state filters:** Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Filipino, Vietnamese, Southeast Asian, South Asian, Native Hawaiian and Pacific Islander; all 50 states, D.C. and the territories. Options with no stories are hidden as you filter.
- **Type filter (News tab):** AAPI Media, Mainstream.
- **Research tab:** news stories and organization statements whose headline or summary mentions new data, a report or study, or a poll or survey, newest first, with the same topic, community, state and source filters. A pill row filters by kind: **Surveys**, **Reports**, **Data** (a poll wins over a report, a report over plain data). Each row says which kind it is, and statements are marked as such. Research is a view across the other tabs: the same stories also stay in News, and statements stay on Advocacy. Policy-tracker records are never listed, and a source typed `research` (AP-NORC) is always listed and appears only here. The matching is by keyword on the headline and summary (patterns in `taxonomy.json` under `research`, with an `ignore` list for look-alikes such as voting "polls", data centers, ethnic studies and the AAPI Data organization's name), so it will list some stories that only touch the subject and miss some that do not name it. Google News rows have a headline only, so fewer of them qualify.
- **Advocacy tab:** statements and press releases from AAPI advocacy organizations (every source with `"kind": "statement"`). They are kept out of News and shown on their own page, with the same topic, community and state filters and a list of groups. Links like `?k=statement` from before this page existed open it.
- **Policy tab (a tracker; official .gov sources, plus state bills from Open States):** congressional bills, Federal Register rules and notices, executive orders and other presidential documents, and statements from government offices. Nothing from news sites. Switch between **All**, **Open for comment** (soonest deadline first), **Federal legislation**, **State legislation** (bills in the 50 states, D.C. and the territories whose title or summary names an AAPI community, from Open States; the pill appears once there are any; each shows its bill number, the state, the latest action and a link to the bill on openstates.org; the **State** filter works on them), **Rules & notices**, **Executive actions**, **Government statements** and **Hearings & reports**. Each record shows its type (for example "H.R. 4001" or "Proposed Rule"), the agency or chamber, its comment deadline, a bill's latest action, and a **Impacts AAPIs** or **Related issue** label. Topic, community, state, source and relevance filters work here too. You can subscribe to an RSS feed of the whole tracker (`data/rss/policy.xml`) or only the open comment periods.
  - *Impacts AAPIs* means the official title or text names Asian American, Native Hawaiian or Pacific Islander communities. *Related issue* means it concerns immigration, language access, the census, civil rights and similar topics that affect these communities without naming them. Both labels come from keyword rules (`taxonomy.json`), so treat them as a filter to start from, not a judgment.
  - Sources: Federal Register API (rules, proposed rules and notices), Federal Register presidential documents (executive actions), Congress.gov API (bills), Regulations.gov API (documents open for comment), GovInfo search API (public laws, hearings, committee reports, GAO reports), and the White House (its Briefings & Statements feed, and its News listing at whitehouse.gov/news: releases, fact sheets, briefings and statements, remarks and research), Justice Department, USCIS and Congressional Asian Pacific American Caucus feeds. Congress.gov, Regulations.gov and GovInfo share one free api.data.gov key. Every record must link to a `.gov` address or it is dropped, except records from a source marked `"official": true` (see Policy sources). Government bodies that do not use a `.gov` address are read this way: the Massachusetts AAPI Commission (aapicommission.org), the Office of Hawaiian Affairs (oha.org), the Oregon Department of Justice and the Illinois governor's newsroom. The other state bodies use `.gov` addresses: the California Commission on Asian and Pacific Islander American Affairs, the California AAPI Legislative Caucus and the Washington Commission on Asian Pacific American Affairs. State governors' and attorneys general's news feeds (California, Washington, Georgia, Oregon and Illinois) and the U.S. Commission on Civil Rights are also read, and for those only items that name the communities are kept. Added 2 Nov 2026: the Department of Hawaiian Home Lands (its media releases and public notices feeds, all kept except commission meeting notices) and the U.S. Department of the Interior (its press-release list at doi.gov/news, which has no feed and is read as a listing page with `"descAfter": true` so the short summary under each headline is also tested; only releases that name the communities or a related issue are kept, and `"backfill"."pageFrom": 1` because its pages are numbered from 0). Interior's Office of Native Hawaiian Relations page (doi.gov/hawaiian) is a static section with no news list, so Native Hawaiian releases reach the Policy tab through the Interior list.
  - The White House News listing has no feed, so the first three pages (about a week of stories) are read from the page itself on each run, and a story is kept only when its headline names AAPI communities or a related issue (the listing carries no summaries, so the headline is all that can be checked). Stories that qualify are tracked for `policyKeepDays` once seen. The presidential-actions pages on whitehouse.gov are deliberately not used. Executive orders, proclamations and memoranda come from the Federal Register instead, which publishes them with numbers and dates in a documented API.
- **Bills in the news:** when a news story names a bill such as "H.R. 1234", the story row shows a link chip to Congress.gov.
- **Saved tab:** star stories on the page to keep them in a list. Saved stories stay in this browser only.
- **Clickable topics and sources:** click any topic or source name in the news list, the details pane or an article to filter by it.
- **Date filter:** on every page (sidebar group *Date*, and a *Date* menu on phones): the last 7 or 30 days, any month of the year, or your own From and To days (either end may be left empty). Day boundaries follow the reader's own time zone. Every group (*Topics*, *Date*, *Communities*, *Relevance*, *Sources*) starts closed on every page, and a group a reader opens or closes stays that way on their next visit.
- **Since your last visit:** the page remembers (in this browser only, never sent anywhere) when you were last here. A visit ends after 30 quiet minutes; whatever was published after the previous visit ended gets a dot beside its time, each tab shows how many new items it has, and a bar above the list offers *Show only new* (also a link: `?n=1`) and *Mark all as seen*. The first visit, or a browser that blocks storage, falls back to the last two days with no bar.
- **Issues and Who's saying what:** the *Issues* tab (`/hubs`, an address kept from when it was called Hubs) has one page per issue (birthright citizenship, Census and race categories, language access and so on; the starter list is `hubs.json`). An issue page gathers every news story, policy item, statement and research report on the issue into one timeline, newest first, with the usual topic, source and date filters. A story belongs to an issue when its headline or summary contains one of the issue's keywords (whole words, any capitals, a plural ending is allowed); the collector works this out on every run and stores only the issue names on the item. Above the timeline, *Who's saying what* lists the organizations that have put out a statement on the issue, each with its latest statement, and underneath the ones that have not (and elected officials, kept apart). Nothing here is guesswork about positions: it only shows who has published something that matches. The site owner can hide starter issues or add their own in the admin dashboard (Settings, Issues). An issue with nothing in it is not shown. Issue pages have their own link (`/hubs?h=birthright-citizenship`).
- **Calendar (events, deadlines, observances and voting dates):** the *Calendar* tab (`/calendar`; the older `/events` and `/elections` addresses still work and lead here or to the pages named under Elections below) puts everything to plan around on one calendar, soonest first, in seven kinds. Four are kept by the site itself, from files you can edit: **observances** and **anniversaries** (`observances.json`: heritage months, days of remembrance, movable holidays and historic dates, each with a link to the source; an anniversary shows how many years it has been, for example "45th anniversary"), **voting dates** (`elections.json`, `dates`: general elections, runoffs and special elections; the kind is called *Voting date*, and the collector keeps every one of them however far ahead it is, unlike the other kinds, which are limited to the look-ahead window), and **comment deadlines** (every open comment period on the Policy tab, taken from the tracker itself). The other three are described next. A **Weeks** view (next to List and Month) groups the next 12 weeks by week, for planning content ahead. In it, in the list, and in the calendar file, a month-long observance is listed once; in the Month grid it appears on its first day only. The kinds: **hearings** (congressional hearings and markups from Congress.gov that match a hub's keywords or an AAPI community, or that have an advocacy organization among the witnesses), **events and webinars** (from the calendar feeds listed under `events.feeds` in `sources.json`, which take any standard `.ics` calendar address, plus events the site owner adds), and **funding and deadlines** (open federal grants from Grants.gov that match the keywords under `events.grants.keywords`, plus fellowship and grant deadlines the owner adds). Buttons filter by kind, a menu filters by topic, a *State* menu narrows the list to one state plus nationwide items (it lists the states that have something on the calendar, and the address records it as `es=`), *Include the past 30 days* shows what just happened, and *Month* switches to a calendar grid (a day can be opened to list just that day). Each item links to its own page (hearing page, source and so on); there is no *Add to calendar* button. The collector still publishes the whole calendar as `data/events.ics` (the page does not link to it; the address can be given to Google, Apple or Outlook Calendar to subscribe). A topic hub page shows a *Coming up* box with the next items on that issue. The tab appears only when something is upcoming. Times are shown in the reader's own time zone; all-day items (deadlines) have no time. The collector (`scripts/events.mjs`, a step of the same update run) refreshes hearings about every 2 hours, grants every 6 hours and calendar feeds every hour, and when a source fails it keeps the last data it had and says so (the status of every source is in `data/events.json`, and in the admin dashboard under Settings, Calendars). Hearings need the free api.data.gov key (`API_DATA_GOV_KEY`, see Optional settings); without it they are skipped and everything else still works. Repeating events in a calendar feed are listed once (the first occurrence), not expanded. A calendar entry that runs longer than `events.maxSpanDays` in `sources.json` (21 days) is left out, so museum exhibitions do not crowd the list; events the owner adds are always kept. The site owner can add events and deadlines, hide any collected item, and add or stop following calendar feeds in the admin dashboard (Settings, Events and funding; Settings, Calendars). Two starter feeds are listed in `sources.json`; if a feed's address stops working the dashboard shows it as not working.
- **Key numbers:** the *Numbers* tab (`/numbers`) is a curated page of statistics on Asian American, Native Hawaiian and Pacific Islander communities: population counts from the Census Bureau, and hate incident and survey figures from the FBI (as reported by Advancing Justice | AAJC), Stop AAPI Hate and California's Attorney General. Each figure shows what it measures, the community and state it covers, the data year and a link to the page where it was published. Buttons filter by kind of figure, menus by community and state, and a box searches. The figures live in `numbers.json`: each entry names its source (organization, title, link, date), a figure that a group reprints from another says so, and nothing on the page is estimated or calculated by the site. An entry whose source link is not `https` is not shown. The `checked` date in the file records when the figures were last checked against their sources (it is not shown on the page). Figures do not update by themselves: when a source publishes new numbers (the Census Bureau's yearly estimates, the FBI's annual report, Stop AAPI Hate's yearly survey), edit the entry and the `checked` date, and add each new figure only after reading it on the source's own page. To add a figure, copy an existing entry and change `id` (letters, digits and dashes), `cat` (`pop`, `biz`, `imm`, `hate` or `vote`; the `vote` figures also feed the Elections page), `v` (the number as it is printed), `t` (what it measures, so that `v` and `t` read as one sentence), `y` (the data year), `s` (a key of `sources`), `p` (the table or page, optional), and optionally `c` (community codes such as `CN` or `SA`), `st` (state codes), `g` (the group's name), `via` and `n` (a caveat).
- **Observances and anniversaries (`observances.json`):** a list the site's editors keep; there is no screen for it in the dashboard, so ask Claude to add or fix an entry, or edit the file. Each entry has `id` (letters, digits and dashes), `k` (`observance` or `anniversary`), `t` (the name), `m` (one or two sentences on why it matters), `u` (a page that confirms the date; it must be `https`), `o` (who publishes that page) and `c` (community codes, or `[]`). The date is one of: `md` (month and day, `"05-01"`, repeating every year), `md` plus `to` (a month-long or multi-day span such as May 1 to 31), `y` (the year it began, for an anniversary, so the page can say "45th anniversary"), or `dates` (a year-by-year list for holidays that move, such as Lunar New Year; the file covers 2026 to 2028, so add 2029 before it runs out). Every fifth anniversary is highlighted as a milestone. To hide one item on the calendar without editing the file, use *Hide* on it in the admin dashboard (the id is `obs-<id>-<year>` or `ann-<id>-<year>`).
- **Voting dates, turnout and ballot measures:** the three parts of the old Elections page now live where the same kind of thing already was. *Voting dates* are on the Calendar (above), from `elections.json` `dates`; each has `id`, `d` (the day, `YYYY-MM-DD`), optional `e` (last day, for a span), `t`, `st` (state codes, or `[]` for nationwide), `n` (a note), `u` (an `https` page that confirms it) and `o` (who publishes it). *Turnout and registration* are on the Numbers page under *Voting and elections*: they are the items in `numbers.json` whose `cat` is `vote` (reported voting and registration rates from the Census Bureau's Current Population Survey, eligible-voter counts from AAPI Data, APIAVote and Pew, and survey results from Advancing Justice | AAJC), like every other figure. *Ballot measures* are on the Policy page as their own kind, *Ballot measures* (`/policy?k=ballot`), from `elections.json` `measures`. They are left out of *All* so they do not crowd out bills and rules; they show under their own button, and in search results. A measure opens in the same reading pane as a bill, with what it would do, how it got on the ballot, the status and the source. Each measure has `id`, `st`, `num` (the ballot number, if the source gives one), `t`, `topic` (one of the topic codes in `taxonomy.json`), `what` (what it would do), `how` (`citizen initiative`, `legislatively referred`, `constitutional amendment` or `veto referendum`), `u`, `o`, and, only when a source ties it to a community, `aapi` (one sentence) with `aapi_u` (that source). The file's `ballotDate` (`2026-11-03`) is the voting day for the list: until then a measure reads *On the ballot <date>*, afterwards *Voted on <date>; the result is not listed here yet*, and once you add `res` to a measure (for example `"Passed with 54%"`) it reads *Result: Passed with 54%*. A row with a missing field, an unknown state or topic, or a link that is not `https` is not shown. The list covers measures the site's reading found in Ballotpedia News as of November 2, 2026; it does not claim a measure affects AAPI communities unless the `aapi` line says so, and no source read for this release tied a state measure to a particular community. Dates and ballot lists change: update `checked` whenever you review them. Old links keep working: `/elections` opens the Calendar filtered to voting dates, `/elections?xs=measures` opens the Policy ballot measures, and `/elections?xs=turnout` opens the Numbers page's voting figures.
- **Election results:** the *Election results* tab (`/results`) lists the 242 AAPI candidates on the 3 Nov 2026 ballot (U.S. Congress, statewide offices and state legislatures) with the Associated Press race call for each: won, lost, runoff or recount, or "Awaiting AP call" until AP calls it (unopposed candidates are labeled). It has totals, a table by office level and party, filters (level, result, state, party, heritage, search), grouped race cards, and a CSV download of the current view. The list, `candidates.json`, was compiled by hand from candidate trackers, Ballotpedia, state election results and community press; it is not an official list, heritage is as sourced, and the page says so. **The site does not get AP data by itself:** AP's race-call feed is a paid, licensed service, so the calls are entered by a person (or by a feed you license) and the page shows them with AP named as the source. Entry is a Google Sheet (template: one row per candidate with a `call` menu, `time` and an apnews.com `link`) published to the web as CSV; put its address in `sources.json` under `results.csv` or in the repository variable `RESULTS_CSV_URL`. Every update run (`scripts/results.mjs`, about every 10 minutes) reads it and writes `data/results.json` (a few KB), and an open Results page rechecks that file every 2 minutes. A call can also be put in `results-calls.csv` in the repository (same columns). Typing mistakes cannot publish a wrong call: an unknown id, a call it does not understand, a time in the future or before election day, and a second winner in a one-seat race for Congress or a statewide office are skipped and shown as warnings in the run's log; an unreadable sheet or a sheet that suddenly holds no calls leaves the published calls as they were. `tools/candidates/build_candidates.py` rebuilds `candidates.json` from the workbook.
- **State policy atlas:** the *State atlas* tab (`/atlas`) compares all 50 states and D.C. in five areas: AAPI history in schools, data broken out by subgroup, language access in state government, hate-crime statutes, and alien land and foreign-ownership laws. A matrix shows each state's status (Enacted, Partial, Bill pending, None found, Unclear) as a word and a shape, not color alone. Selecting any entry opens the law's citation, a one-line summary, the year, links to the sources (primary sources marked), a verbatim quote and the date it was verified; one area can be focused, filtered by status, searched by state name or postal code, and downloaded as a CSV. It is a **snapshot checked on 2 Nov 2026, not a live feed, and not legal advice**: "None found" means the sources checked showed no such law, not that none exists, and the page says where a primary source could not be reached. The quotes were checked against the cited pages by `tools/atlas/qcheck.py`: 129 of 138 matched, and the entry says when one did not. Data, build script and refresh steps are in `tools/atlas/`; the page loads `atlas.json` (about 215 KB) only when opened.
- **Power map:** the *Power map* tab (`/power`) covers public officials and institutions only, in five sections: **Congress** (members of the 119th Congress whom a cited source identifies as Asian American, Native Hawaiian or Pacific Islander, each with the basis for listing them, never inferred from a name or photo, and a link to their Congress.gov page, where the bills they sponsor are listed; plus the Congressional Asian Pacific American Caucus and its members), **Commissions and caucuses** (federal, state, territory and city bodies on AAPI affairs and legislative caucuses, with status), **Coalitions** (such as NCAPA, with members linked to their Organizations profile where one exists), **Court cases** (organizations as parties, counsel or friends of the court, with the latest verified event), and **Funding** (grants of $5,000 or more reported on IRS filings by 18 foundations, matched to directory organizations, each linked to its filing on ProPublica; it is partial and says so). Organizations' statements on bills stay on the Issues pages (*Who's saying what*) and are not copied. It is a dated snapshot with a source for every entry, searchable, and the page loads `power.json` (about 180 KB) only when opened. Data, build script and refresh steps are in `tools/power/`.
- **Organizations:** the *Organizations* tab (`/orgs`) is a directory of 73 AAPI organizations, agencies and media outlets, each with a profile page (`/orgs?og=<id>`). A profile gives where the group works (national, regional, state or local, and its headquarters city), the communities and issues it focuses on, and a link to its site. Where the site follows the group's own feed, the profile also lists what it has said here (statements, reports, news) with the count for the last 90 days, links to the topic pages for its issues, and any upcoming events it is hosting; a media outlet shows its recent stories instead. Buttons and menus filter by community, issue, kind of organization and state; the list can be sorted A to Z or by recent activity. The directory lives in `orgs.json`: each entry has `id` (letters, digits and dashes), `n` (name), `ab` (a short description of the group, kept in the file but not shown on the site), `u` (the group's site, `https`), `sc` (`national`, `regional`, `state`, `local` or `government`), `hq` (city and state, or blank), `st` (state codes for a state or local group), `c` (community codes, or `[]` for pan-AAPI groups), `is` (topic codes), `ty` (the kind of organization), `o` (the page the description was written from) and optionally `src` (the exact source names the group appears under in the feed, so its items are counted and listed) and `fd` (year founded). 20 entries have no headquarters listed because their own sites do not give one; add `hq` when you confirm it.
- **Shareable links:** the current filters are kept in the page address (`?t=…&c=…&st=…&k=…&s=…&d=…&q=…`).
- **Feeds (generated, but not linked from the page):** an RSS feed for every topic, community, state and type, one for the Policy tracker, one for comment deadlines, a calendar file (`data/deadlines.ics`), and an optional weekday email brief that lists new policy records and comment deadlines.

## How it works
A GitHub Action runs `scripts/fetch.mjs` about every 10 minutes (`.github/workflows/tick.yml` checks every 5 minutes and starts `update.yml` when the last update began 7 or more minutes ago, because GitHub starts scheduled runs late and sometimes skips one), and a page that is left open checks for new data every 10 minutes. GitHub's scheduler can still be late or skip a run, so `tools/cloudflare-trigger/` has an optional free Cloudflare Worker that starts the update on an exact 10-minute timer. It pulls the feeds in `sources.json`, drops non-English and abroad-only items, drops off-topic items from general outlets, de-duplicates, tags each story, and commits `data/feed.json`. Everything published on or after **`keepFrom` (1 January 2026)** is kept for good, from every source, and is never counted against a cap; older stories follow the usual rules: News is kept 30 days, Policy records 120 days (an open comment period is kept until its deadline passes). A policy record is dropped unless it links to a `.gov` address. `scripts/build-outputs.mjs` then writes the RSS feeds and calendar. `index.html` is a static page that reads those files.

Nothing is generated or rewritten. Text that looks auto-trimmed from an article body is dropped, and a description appears only when the publisher supplied one.

## Deploy (free, about 10 minutes)
1. Create a **public** GitHub repo and upload everything here, including the hidden `.github` folder.
2. Settings → Actions → General → Workflow permissions → **Read and write**.
3. Settings → Pages → Deploy from branch → `main` / root.
4. Actions tab → **update-feed** → Run workflow. Open the run log: each source prints OK or FAIL.
5. Visit `https://<your-username>.github.io/<repo>/`.

Cloudflare Pages and Netlify also work: connect the repo, no build command, publish directory `/`.

## Page addresses
The dashboard (welcome page) is `/`, the news list is `/news`, and the other pages are `/policy`, `/advocacy`, `/research`, `/hubs`, `/calendar`, `/results`, `/atlas`, `/power`, `/numbers`, `/orgs` and `/saved` (older `/#v/policy` style links still work and are rewritten to the plain address; a link to the old front page with filters in it, such as `/?q=visa` or `/?t=HEALTH`, now opens `/news` with them, and a story link `/#a/<id>` still opens the story). An article opens as `#a/<id>` on top of its page, for example `/policy#a/abc123`; the "Copy link" button copies the story's address at its source (a Yappie article copies its theyappie.com address, not the page on this site). GitHub Pages serves `policy.html` at `/policy`, so the repo holds `news.html`, `policy.html`, `advocacy.html`, `research.html`, `hubs.html`, `calendar.html`, `results.html`, `atlas.html`, `power.html`, `numbers.html`, `orgs.html` and `saved.html` as copies of `index.html` (`events.html` and `elections.html` are kept only so old links still reach the app), and `404.html` is another copy that answers any other address (for example `/policy/`) and corrects it. After editing `index.html`, run `bash scripts/pages.sh .` to refresh the copies. On a host that rewrites every address to `index.html` (Netlify `/* /index.html 200`, Cloudflare Pages) the copies are not needed.

## Admin dashboard
`tools/admin/` is a password-protected dashboard (one free Cloudflare Worker) where you log in and approve, edit, retag, keep or delete any item on the site, add your own stories, and choose whether new stories wait for approval. From its Settings tab you can also rename issues, news sources, collected events, topics, communities and sections (Names), add, change, hide or remove organizations on the Organizations tab and figures on the Numbers tab (Organizations and Numbers; `orgs.json` and `numbers.json` are the starter lists, and the dashboard's changes are published as `data/orgs.json` and `data/numbers.json`), add your own topics and communities (with keywords, so new stories are tagged automatically), rename or remove the built-in topics, communities and News categories (a removed one can be restored; the catch-all topic, Culture, can be renamed but not removed), put each news source in AAPI Media, Mainstream or a category you create, file a story under more than one topic (a main topic plus up to four extra ones, set by hand in Edit or Retag), and set Policy items to *Impacts AAPIs* or *Related issue* and Research items to *Report*, *Survey* or *Data*. The names of your own topics, communities and categories, and which built-in ones are renamed (`rn`) or removed (`off`), are published in the feed (`taxo`); their keywords stay private. An item with extra topics carries them in `tx`; the topic filter, search, RSS feeds and email digest treat a story as belonging to every one of its topics. It saves your decisions (`admin.json`) to a separate **private** repository, `theyappie/yappie-pro-admin`, and the collector writes stories waiting for approval (`pending.json`) there too, so nothing unpublished is ever on this public site. The collector applies the decisions on its next run. Set-up steps (including the private repository and its token), how the login is protected, and the limits are in `tools/admin/README.md`. **Blocked sources** (Settings): type a source, confirm, and all of its stories leave the site and nothing new is collected from it; they are not put in the Trash, and unblocking brings the source back from the next update (its older stories after the next full scan). **Featured** (the Feature button on any item, the bulk bar, the Add form, or Settings): featured items lead their page, newest featured first, with a *Featured* label, with no limit, and they are kept past the usual number of days. **Breaking news** (the Breaking button, the bulk bar, the Add form, or Settings): mark an item as *Breaking*, *Urgent* or *Important* for 2 hours to 3 days; it moves to the top of its page in red with a flashing label until the time is up, then returns to its usual place and look by itself (the page checks the clock, so nobody has to reload; reduced-motion settings turn the flashing off). The feed carries these as `fe` (featured time) and `bt`, `bu`, `bk` (marked at, until, label).

## Customize
- **Blocklist, country domains, routes and relevance:** **What is kept and where it goes** (all set in `sources.json`): `excludeHosts` and `excludeNames` list sites and publishers that are never shown, with their subdomains. Any site on a country domain outside the U.S. (.ca, .ch, .uz, .co.uk, .com.au and every other two-letter country ending, except .us and the U.S. territories .gu .as .mp .vi .pr .um) is blocked too, and a source on one is not fetched; set `"blockForeignDomains": false` to turn that off, or list a site in `allowHosts` to let it through. `gatedHosts` lets through only some of an allowed site's stories: rnz.co.nz (RNZ Pacific) is allowed, but a story from it (by its address, by the address Google News reports for its publisher, or by its name "RNZ") is kept only when its headline or summary names Pacific Islander people or places (`require`) and does not read as sport (`deny`: rugby, league, cup, netball, athletes, "Tonga beats Samoa" and the like). A sports word list can miss a match report that uses none of them; add the word to `deny`. independent.co.uk (The Independent, a U.K. paper on a .co.uk address) is allowed with `"community": true` in its gate, which keeps a story only when its headline or summary names the communities in `communityPattern` (Asian Americans, Pacific Islanders, Native Hawaiians and the rest); its Pacific Islands and Asian Americans topic feeds, two U.S. section feeds and a Google News search are read, and everything else it publishes is dropped. A gate can use `require` (a pattern), `community`, or both (either one passes), and `deny` always drops. A story that passes its gate skips the headline test. A story that links to a .gov address, or whose Google News publisher is a .gov site, moves from News to the Policy page on its own. `routes` sends a named site or publisher to another page (asianlawcaucus.org and aclu.org go to Advocacy, csis.org goes to Research). `renames` maps a source or publisher name to the name shown on the page ("AP News" is shown as "Associated Press", "guampdn.com" as "Pacific Daily News", "TAAF" as "The Asian American Foundation", "CAPAC" as "Congressional Asian Pacific American Caucus", and so on); it is applied to new and stored stories by the collector and to older feed files by the page, so a name is changed in one place. Broad Google News searches can return stories that only share a word with the communities (a Guam soccer score, a stock ticker), so a story found that way is shown only if its headline names the communities (`communityPattern`) or its publisher is trusted: every source in the list plus `trustedOutlets` (local Guam, Hawaii and Pacific outlets and U.S. community papers). Remove an outlet from `trustedOutlets` to hold it to the headline test as well. Stored stories are checked by the same rules on every update. Stories about Vietnamese Americans, Taiwanese Americans and the other communities named in `communityPattern` are never treated as news from abroad. linkedin.com and britannica.com are blocked as non-news.
- **Pew Research Center (Research tab):** six sources read pewresearch.org's own WordPress API (its topic feeds all return the same site-wide feed): the Asian Americans topic (reports and short reads, no filter), the Race & Ethnicity topic and two searches for "Pacific Islander", each kept only when the headline or summary names Asian American, Native Hawaiian or Pacific Islander communities (`filter` plus the source's own `match` pattern, which also knows "Asians in the U.S."). Report parts (Acknowledgments, Methodology, Appendix, numbered chapters) are skipped. Pew publishes a few such reports a year, so `"keepDays": 730` on those sources keeps their stories for two years instead of the 30-day default, and research stories are counted apart from the news cap (`researchMax`, 150). A source can set its own `keepDays` and `match`.
- **Pacific Islander coverage:** the "Google News: Pacific Islands" search looks for Pacific Islands, Pacific Islanders and Pasifika stories about climate, sea level, deep-sea mining, sovereignty, nuclear legacy, migration and the Compact of Free Association. `communityPattern` now also recognises "Pacific islands", Pasifika, Pacific peoples and communities, Polynesian, Melanesian, the Marshall Islands and the Cook Islands, so such a story passes the headline test. A site on a country domain such as independent.co.uk stays blocked.
- **Pacific island nations and trip stories (News tab):** this site is about Pacific Islander communities in the United States, so a news story that is only about a Pacific island country (Fiji's constitution vote, a Samoan prime minister on Israel, a drug-detection unit in Tonga) is dropped from every source. A story is kept when its headline or summary has a tie to the U.S.: the U.S. government, military or Congress, the Compact of Free Association, nuclear testing, climate change and sea-level rise, the U.S. territories (Guam, the Marianas, American Samoa), Hawaii and the other states, or the communities themselves (`usTie` and `usTieCase`, which are matched on the headline and summary; `pacificNations` names the countries and regional bodies). A story from a newspaper or station on U.S. soil (`usOutlets`: Guam, the Marianas, American Samoa and Hawaii outlets) is never dropped this way, and neither is a story you pinned or featured. RNZ's gate also requires a tie (`"tie": true`). A trip or celebrity story that uses a Pacific place only as a destination (a TV star's exhausting trip to Guam, a K-pop idol's Hawaii honeymoon) is dropped when it names no community (`travelPlaces` with `travelWords`). Both rules apply to stored stories on every update, and the run log lists what was dropped and why. To keep a kind of story, add its words to `usTie`; to stop a kind, add them to `pacificNations` or `travelWords`.
- **Sources:** edit `sources.json`. `"filter": true` keeps only AAPI-relevant stories from general outlets. `"kind"` sets the type: `community` (shown as AAPI Media), `news` (Mainstream, the default for any source without a kind), `statement`, `research`. `"google": true` marks a Google News RSS search (personal, non-commercial use under Google's terms). `"fullText": true` saves article text for the reader (use it only for content you own or have rights to; the feed must include full post text). Set `"enabled": false` to switch a source off. `excludeHosts` lists sites that are never shown, with their subdomains (currently newsletters.theyappie.com, hoodline.com, vietnam.vn, dainikjagranmpcg.com, kelownadailycourier.ca, nationalenquirer.co and .com, sofascore.com, usresistnews.org, constitutioncenter.org, history.com and medium.com). Google News shows a publisher's name rather than its address, so each blocked site's name is also matched ("hoodline.com" blocks a publisher called Hoodline), and `excludeNames` adds names that differ from the address (for example The National Constitution Center). Stories already stored from a blocked site are removed on the next update.
- **Site name and icons:** the site is named "The Yappie Pro — Informing and Empowering AAPI Communities". That name is the tab title of every page, the `application-name` and `og:site_name` tags in the page head, and the `name` in `site.webmanifest`; a story's tab title is its headline followed by "The Yappie Pro". The icons in the site root (`favicon.ico` with 16, 32 and 48 px, `favicon-32.png`, `apple-touch-icon.png`, `icon-192.png`, `icon-512.png`) come from the Yappie "y" tile: the full tile for the large sizes and a tighter crop of the same art for the tab sizes, so the letter stays readable at 16 px. The admin dashboard serves these same three tab icons (`favicon.ico`, `favicon-32.png`, `apple-touch-icon.png`); `tools/admin/build.mjs` embeds them in `worker.js`, so run it again after changing them. `policy.html`, `advocacy.html`, `research.html`, `saved.html` and `404.html` are copies of `index.html`, so a change to the head goes into all of them.
- **Which Yappie stories are listed:** every story on theyappie.com published on or after **1 January 2026**, except those in the **Press** or **Newsletters** categories (the newsletter issues live on newsletters.theyappie.com, which is blocked as a site as well). Three options on The Yappie's entry in `sources.json` do this, and any source can use them: `"excludeCategories": ["Press", "Newsletters"]` drops a story that has one of those categories in its feed, whatever its other categories are and however the name is capitalised (a copy stored by an earlier run is removed too); `"pages": 10` reads up to that many pages of a WordPress feed (`feed/?paged=2`, 10 stories a page) and stops at the first page that reaches back past the days the source's stories are kept, so the older stories come in on the first run and later runs only need page 1 in practice; `"keepSince": "YYYY-MM-DD"` keeps one source's stories published on or after that day (UTC) however old they get, instead of the usual 30 days (`keepDays`), outside the news cap (`maxItems`). The Yappie no longer needs it: `keepFrom` (below) keeps its stories from 1 January 2026, and its history is read by paging its feed (see **All of 2026** below). To change the categories, edit that entry.
- **Yappie article page:** the byline author is a link to the author's page on theyappie.com (`/author/<name>/`). That address cannot be worked out from the name, so the collector reads it from the story's own page (the link whose text is the author's name), stores it as `byu` in `data/articles/<id>.json`, and reads each new author once (at most 6 pages per run). A byline whose stories live on another site, such as "The Yappie Staff" (newsletter stories), is listed under `"authors"` on The Yappie's entry in `sources.json`. An author with no page stays plain text. The article page shows no community chips.
- **Fonts:** the Yappie article viewer uses Corsario Variable for its headline and story text, and everything else (feed headlines, the details pane, chips) uses Tablet Gothic, both from an Adobe Fonts web project (the `<link>` near the top of `index.html`; the font files stay on Adobe's servers, because their terms do not allow self-hosting). IBM Plex Sans from Google Fonts is the backup if Adobe cannot be reached. To change fonts, edit that link and the `--head` (article viewer) and `--sans` (everything else) lines at the top of the page's CSS. Tablet Gothic is in the project as regular and bold only, so medium and semibold text shows as regular or bold; feed headlines and the grouped "More ... stories" headlines are set to regular.
- **Ways to read a source:** `"feed"` (RSS or Atom), `"wp"` (a WordPress REST list, for example `https://aapidata.com/wp-json/wp/v2/posts?categories=13&per_page=20&_fields=link,title,date_gmt,excerpt`), or `"page"` (an organization's own listing page; `"link"` is a regular expression that the path of each story link must match). A story found on a page needs a title and a date on that page, otherwise it is left out, and the page is read once per run even when two sources use it. Optional settings help with pages that do not list dates plainly or lay stories out unusually: `"embedded": {"title": …, "link": …, "date": …}` reads stories from JSON that the page carries in a `<script>` tag (used for the South China Morning Post topic page; dates may be ISO text or epoch seconds or milliseconds), `"idDate"` is a regular expression whose first group, found in the story link, is a 24-character story ID that begins with its creation time (used for HuffPost, which prints no dates), `"before": true` reads a listing of cards that show the date and headline above a "Read more" button (APIAHF, NAPCA), and `"attr"` keeps only links whose own attributes match a pattern (used to read HuffPost's main story list and not the ad module of other stories beside it). `"skipTitle"` on a news source drops results whose headline matches it, for pages that are not stories (see Nichi Bei below).
- **All of 2026 (`keepFrom`) and the backfill:** `"keepFrom": "2026-01-01"` in `sources.json` keeps every story, statement, government record and research item published on or after that day, from every source: nothing is aged out after 30 or 120 days and nothing counts against `maxItems`, `researchMax` or `policyMax` (those caps now apply only to older items). Remove the line to go back to a rolling window; change the date for a different year. A feed shows only its newest 10 to 50 stories, so a one-off **backfill** reads each source back to `keepFrom`: Actions → **update-feed** → Run workflow → tick **backfill** (`only` can name sources, for example `Civil Beat|HuffPost`, as a regular expression). It takes up to about two hours (the normal 10-minute updates wait meanwhile), and it is safe to run again: it only adds what is missing. The run log ends with one line per source (`OK` when its oldest story reaches 2026-01, `SHORT` when it stopped earlier, with the reason). `scripts/backfill.mjs` reads each site the way that site allows: a WordPress site's REST list (100 stories a request, only those after `keepFrom`; found from the feed's address), a feed's own paging (`?paged=2`), Blogger, Substack and Squarespace paging, the numbered pages of a listing page, a feed that takes a date range (the Justice Department), and, for sites with none of these, **Google News searches by date** (`site:example.com after:… before:…`; a window that returns 90 stories or more is split in two, down to a single day). A source can carry its own `"backfill"` hints (`modes`, `pages`, `site`, `terms`, `json`, `sliced`; `false` skips it) and the top-level `"backfill"` block sets pacing (`delayMs`), retries, the Google window sizes and a `deadlineMinutes` limit after which no new request is started. **What a backfill does differently:** stories it finds go live even when new stories wait for approval in the admin dashboard (a story that already waits, or that you held back, stays as it is); the government sources are read from `keepFrom` with bigger pages (`backfill.gov`: Federal Register and executive actions, Congress.gov bills updated since then, Regulations.gov documents posted since then, GovInfo), and rules that touch a related issue are listed even after their comment period closed (a related-issue Regulations.gov notice is left out, there are too many). A normal run afterwards is unchanged. Stories before `keepFrom` that are still in a feed follow the old 30-day rule.
- **Feed size and speed:** a year of stories is a feed of several MB (about 8,000 stories is 4 MB, about 1 MB sent compressed). The page groups stories about the same event only within 3 days of each other and keeps each story's search text after the first search, so a feed of that size opens in about a second. Each RSS feed lists its newest `rssMax` stories (`sources.json`, 200); the comment-deadline feed and calendar list only comment periods that are still open. The same headline is treated as one story only within 12 days, so a recurring title ("Weekly roundup") months apart is kept.

## Things to know
- Topic, community, state and bill tags come from keyword matching on the headline and excerpt. They are fast and transparent but not perfect: a story can be tagged with a topic it only touches, and a story can miss a tag. The page has no About tab, so these limits are written down here.
- The English check is a word-list heuristic. It catches nearly all Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Vietnamese and other non-English headlines, and most Spanish ones, but a short Spanish headline can slip through.
- **Live check, 31 Oct 2026:** the collector was run once against the real Federal Register, Congress.gov, Regulations.gov, GovInfo, White House, Justice Department, USCIS and CAPAC servers from a cloud sandbox, and each answered in the expected format. USCIS sits behind a bot filter that answered one test request with 403, so it may be blocked from some networks. Three news-side feeds (NextShark, Asian Americans Advancing Justice, OCA) returned 404 and need new addresses. Nothing has been run from GitHub Actions itself. Fix or disable any source that shows FAIL in your first run log.
- **Boilerplate is filtered on purpose.** `sources.json` has `skipTitle` patterns that drop museum repatriation notices (they mention Native Hawaiian organizations but are not policy), paperwork-collection notices, ceremonial observances such as "Labor Day, 2026" when they are found only through an issue word, and individual court cases in USCIS and Justice Department releases unless they name the communities. Edit or remove a pattern if you want those items.
- **Titles decide for Regulations.gov and GovInfo.** Their list views carry no summary, so an item is kept only when its title names the communities or (for "Related issue") one of the issue words. Regulations.gov items that the Federal Register query already lists are not repeated.
- "Impacts AAPIs" and "Related issue" are keyword heuristics. Expect some records labeled "Related issue" that matter little, and some that matter but are missed. How records qualify: a rule or notice is kept if it names AAPI communities, or if it is a rule on a related issue whose comment period is still open. An executive action is kept if the Federal Register's search finds it through an AAPI term ("Impacts AAPIs") or an issue term ("Related issue"). A bill is "Impacts AAPIs" if its title or Congress.gov summary does, and "Related issue" if its title touches one of the issue words. Hearings and reports (kind "Hearings & reports") and public laws from GovInfo qualify the same way, on their titles.
- Not built: district-level filtering, hearing and court-docket feeds, and Spanish or other-language headlines. The State atlas and Power map are snapshots that a person refreshes (see `tools/atlas/` and `tools/power/`); they do not update on their own.

## Free-tier limits (what keeps this free)
- **GitHub Actions:** this is a public repository, so standard runners are free and unmetered. The update job runs about every 10 minutes and takes a minute or two; nothing else is scheduled except the weekday brief. If the repository is ever made private, the free allowance is 2,000 minutes a month and the update would have to be slowed to about once an hour.
- **GitHub Pages:** the site is static (about 250 KB of page plus data files, loaded on demand). Pages' soft limits are a 1 GB site, 100 GB of traffic a month and about 10 builds an hour. The update normally commits every 10 minutes (about 6 builds an hour) and never starts closer than 7 minutes after the last one (at most 8 an hour), so it stays under the build limit; a few hand-made pushes in the same hour are fine, and if the limit is ever hit a build is simply replaced by the next commit's. Pages deploys the latest commit, so a cancelled build is not a failed site.
- **Repository size:** each update that changes data adds roughly 20 KB to the repository after Git packs it, about 1 GB a year at full speed. GitHub asks that repositories stay under about 1 GB (it warns well before it blocks). When the repository nears 1 GB, squash the history: create an orphan branch from `main`, commit once, force-push it as `main`, and the history resets to a few MB (the site and data are unchanged; open pull requests and the commit log are lost).
- **Cloudflare:** visitors never touch Cloudflare unless you host the site there. The only Cloudflare pieces are the optional admin dashboard Worker (`tools/admin/`) and the optional update timer (`tools/cloudflare-trigger/`), each within the free Workers allowance of 100,000 requests a day (the dashboard is used by one person; the timer runs 144 times a day).
- **Claude:** the site has no runtime use of Claude. Claude is used only when someone asks it to edit the site or refresh a snapshot, and that counts against the person's own Claude plan, not the site.
- **API keys:** `API_DATA_GOV_KEY` (about 120 requests an hour against roughly 1,000 allowed) and `OPENSTATES_API_KEY` (at most 12 requests every 3 hours) stay inside their free limits; the run log prints the usage.
- **New pages cost nothing at rest:** the Election results, State atlas and Power map files are fetched only when a visitor opens that page, are served from Pages' cache, and are never rebuilt by the update job, except `data/results.json` (a few KB, rewritten only when a race call changes).
