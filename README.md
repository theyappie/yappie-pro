# The Yappie Pro

A free, open-source news and policy terminal for stories affecting Asian American, Native Hawaiian and Pacific Islander (AAPI) communities in the U.S. No ads, no tracking, no accounts, no server, no business model.

## What it does
- **News tab** from community outlets, general outlets (filtered to AAPI-relevant stories) and, optionally, Google News searches. **English-language items only.** Each item is a headline: clicking or tapping it opens a details pane (a full-screen sheet on phones) with an "Open source" button that goes to the original; headlines are not direct links. The page shows no summaries: excerpts are used only to tag stories (topic, community, state); the one exception is the subtitle on The Yappie's own stories.
- **The Yappie reader:** stories hosted on theyappie.com open in an in-portal article view. One click on desktop, one tap on a phone.
- **Issue topics:** Immigration, Civil Rights, Health, Elections, Economy, Housing, Education, Courts, Government, Pacific, Culture.
- **Community and state filters:** Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Filipino, Vietnamese, Southeast Asian, South Asian, Native Hawaiian and Pacific Islander; all 50 states, D.C. and the territories. Options with no stories are hidden as you filter.
- **Type filter (News tab):** AAPI Media, Mainstream.
- **Research tab:** news stories and organization statements whose headline or summary mentions new data, a report or study, or a poll or survey, newest first, with the same topic, community, state and source filters. A pill row filters by kind: **Surveys**, **Reports**, **Data** (a poll wins over a report, a report over plain data). Each row says which kind it is, and statements are marked as such. Research is a view across the other tabs: the same stories also stay in News, and statements stay on Advocacy. Policy-tracker records are never listed, and a source typed `research` (AP-NORC) is always listed and appears only here. The matching is by keyword on the headline and summary (patterns in `taxonomy.json` under `research`, with an `ignore` list for look-alikes such as voting "polls", data centers, ethnic studies and the AAPI Data organization's name), so it will list some stories that only touch the subject and miss some that do not name it. Google News rows have a headline only, so fewer of them qualify.
- **Advocacy tab:** statements and press releases from AAPI advocacy organizations (every source with `"kind": "statement"`). They are kept out of News and shown on their own page, with the same topic, community and state filters and a list of groups. Links like `?k=statement` from before this page existed open it.
- **Policy tab (a tracker, .gov sources only):** congressional bills, Federal Register rules and notices, executive orders and other presidential documents, and statements from government offices. Nothing from news sites. Switch between **All**, **Open for comment** (soonest deadline first), **Legislation**, **Rules & notices**, **Executive actions**, **Government statements** and **Hearings & reports**. Each record shows its type (for example "H.R. 4001" or "Proposed Rule"), the agency or chamber, its comment deadline, a bill's latest action, and a **Impacts AAPIs** or **Related issue** label. Topic, community, state, source and relevance filters work here too. You can subscribe to an RSS feed of the whole tracker (`data/rss/policy.xml`) or only the open comment periods.
  - *Impacts AAPIs* means the official title or text names Asian American, Native Hawaiian or Pacific Islander communities. *Related issue* means it concerns immigration, language access, the census, civil rights and similar topics that affect these communities without naming them. Both labels come from keyword rules (`taxonomy.json`), so treat them as a filter to start from, not a judgment.
  - Sources: Federal Register API (rules, proposed rules and notices), Federal Register presidential documents (executive actions), Congress.gov API (bills), Regulations.gov API (documents open for comment), GovInfo search API (public laws, hearings, committee reports, GAO reports), and the White House (its Briefings & Statements feed, and its News listing at whitehouse.gov/news: releases, fact sheets, briefings and statements, remarks and research), Justice Department, USCIS and Congressional Asian Pacific American Caucus feeds. Congress.gov, Regulations.gov and GovInfo share one free api.data.gov key. Every record must link to a `.gov` address or it is dropped.
  - The White House News listing has no feed, so the first three pages (about a week of stories) are read from the page itself on each run, and a story is kept only when its headline names AAPI communities or a related issue (the listing carries no summaries, so the headline is all that can be checked). Stories that qualify are tracked for `policyKeepDays` once seen. The presidential-actions pages on whitehouse.gov are deliberately not used. Executive orders, proclamations and memoranda come from the Federal Register instead, which publishes them with numbers and dates in a documented API.
- **Bills in the news:** when a news story names a bill such as "H.R. 1234", the story row shows a link chip to Congress.gov.
- **Saved tab:** star stories on the page to keep them in a list. Saved stories stay in this browser only.
- **Clickable topics and sources:** click any topic or source name in the news list, the details pane or an article to filter by it.
- **Shareable links:** the current filters are kept in the page address (`?t=…&c=…&st=…&k=…&s=…&q=…`).
- **Feeds (generated, but not linked from the page):** an RSS feed for every topic, community, state and type, one for the Policy tracker, one for comment deadlines, a calendar file (`data/deadlines.ics`), and an optional weekday email brief that lists new policy records and comment deadlines.

## How it works
A GitHub Action runs `scripts/fetch.mjs` about every 10 minutes (`.github/workflows/tick.yml` checks every 5 minutes and starts `update.yml` when the last update began 7 or more minutes ago, because GitHub starts scheduled runs late and sometimes skips one), and a page that is left open checks for new data every 10 minutes. GitHub's scheduler can still be late or skip a run, so `tools/cloudflare-trigger/` has an optional free Cloudflare Worker that starts the update on an exact 10-minute timer. It pulls the feeds in `sources.json`, drops non-English and abroad-only items, drops off-topic items from general outlets, de-duplicates, tags each story, and commits `data/feed.json`. News is kept 30 days. Policy records are kept 120 days (an open comment period is kept until its deadline passes), and a policy record is dropped unless it links to a `.gov` address. `scripts/build-outputs.mjs` then writes the RSS feeds and calendar. `index.html` is a static page that reads those files.

Nothing is generated or rewritten. Text that looks auto-trimmed from an article body is dropped, and a description appears only when the publisher supplied one.

## Deploy (free, about 10 minutes)
1. Create a **public** GitHub repo and upload everything here, including the hidden `.github` folder.
2. Settings → Actions → General → Workflow permissions → **Read and write**.
3. Settings → Pages → Deploy from branch → `main` / root.
4. Actions tab → **update-feed** → Run workflow. Open the run log: each source prints OK or FAIL.
5. Visit `https://<your-username>.github.io/<repo>/`.

Cloudflare Pages and Netlify also work: connect the repo, no build command, publish directory `/`.

## Page addresses
News is `/`, and the other pages are `/policy`, `/advocacy`, `/research` and `/saved` (older `/#v/policy` style links still work and are rewritten to the plain address). An article opens as `#a/<id>` on top of its page, for example `/policy#a/abc123`; the "Copy link" button copies the story's address at its source (a Yappie article copies its theyappie.com address, not the page on this site). GitHub Pages serves `policy.html` at `/policy`, so the repo holds `policy.html`, `advocacy.html`, `research.html` and `saved.html` as copies of `index.html`, and `404.html` is another copy that answers any other address (for example `/policy/`) and corrects it. After editing `index.html`, run `bash scripts/pages.sh .` to refresh the copies. On a host that rewrites every address to `index.html` (Netlify `/* /index.html 200`, Cloudflare Pages) the copies are not needed.

## Admin dashboard
`tools/admin/` is a password-protected dashboard (one free Cloudflare Worker) where you log in and approve, edit, retag, keep or delete any item on the site, add your own stories, and choose whether new stories wait for approval. From its Settings tab you can also add your own topics and communities (with keywords, so new stories are tagged automatically), put each news source in AAPI Media, Mainstream or a category you create, and set Policy items to *Impacts AAPIs* or *Related issue* and Research items to *Report*, *Survey* or *Data*. The names of your own topics, communities and categories are published in the feed (`taxo`); their keywords stay private. It saves your decisions (`admin.json`) to a separate **private** repository, `theyappie/yappie-pro-admin`, and the collector writes stories waiting for approval (`pending.json`) there too, so nothing unpublished is ever on this public site. The collector applies the decisions on its next run. Set-up steps (including the private repository and its token), how the login is protected, and the limits are in `tools/admin/README.md`.

## Optional settings
| Setting | Where | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `API_DATA_GOV_KEY` | Repo secret | One free api.data.gov key (https://api.congress.gov/sign-up/) that works for Congress.gov (bills), Regulations.gov (documents open for comment) and GovInfo (public laws, hearings, committee reports, GAO reports). The older name `CONGRESS_API_KEY` is still read. Without a key the rest of the tracker still works, the run log says "skipped" for those three sources, and the Policy tab lists them under "Not loaded this time". The key is sent in a request header (never in a URL) and is never printed or stored. **Request budget:** each run makes at most `apiBudget.perRun` key requests (150; a normal run uses about 20) and stops early when a service reports that less than `apiBudget.keepFraction` (25%) of its hourly allowance is left. The job runs about every 10 minutes (at most 6 times an hour, so roughly 120 requests an hour), which stays far below the roughly 1,000 requests an hour each service allows per key. The run log prints "API key requests this run: N of 150 allowed". |
| `SMTP_URL`, `DIGEST_FROM`, `DIGEST_TO` | Repo secrets | Turns on the weekday email brief (`daily-digest` workflow, 12:30 UTC). `DIGEST_TO` is comma-separated and sent as BCC. Without them, the brief is still written to `data/digest/latest.html`. |
| `SITE_URL`, `DIGEST_TOPICS` | Repo variables | Your public portal URL (used in feeds and the brief) and an optional topic filter for the brief, e.g. `IMMIGRATION,ELECTIONS`. |

## Customize
- **Blocklist, country domains, routes and relevance:** **What is kept and where it goes** (all set in `sources.json`): `excludeHosts` and `excludeNames` list sites and publishers that are never shown, with their subdomains. Any site on a country domain outside the U.S. (.ca, .ch, .uz, .co.uk, .com.au and every other two-letter country ending, except .us and the U.S. territories .gu .as .mp .vi .pr .um) is blocked too, and a source on one is not fetched; set `"blockForeignDomains": false` to turn that off, or list a site in `allowHosts` to let it through. `gatedHosts` lets through only some of an allowed site's stories: rnz.co.nz (RNZ Pacific) is allowed, but a story from it (by its address, by the address Google News reports for its publisher, or by its name "RNZ") is kept only when its headline or summary names Pacific Islander people or places (`require`) and does not read as sport (`deny`: rugby, league, cup, netball, athletes, "Tonga beats Samoa" and the like). A sports word list can miss a match report that uses none of them; add the word to `deny`. independent.co.uk (The Independent, a U.K. paper on a .co.uk address) is allowed with `"community": true` in its gate, which keeps a story only when its headline or summary names the communities in `communityPattern` (Asian Americans, Pacific Islanders, Native Hawaiians and the rest); its Pacific Islands and Asian Americans topic feeds, two U.S. section feeds and a Google News search are read, and everything else it publishes is dropped. A gate can use `require` (a pattern), `community`, or both (either one passes), and `deny` always drops. A story that passes its gate skips the headline test. A story that links to a .gov address, or whose Google News publisher is a .gov site, moves from News to the Policy page on its own. `routes` sends a named site or publisher to another page (asianlawcaucus.org and aclu.org go to Advocacy, csis.org goes to Research). `renames` maps a source or publisher name to the name shown on the page ("AP News" is shown as "Associated Press", "guampdn.com" as "Pacific Daily News", "TAAF" as "The Asian American Foundation", "CAPAC" as "Congressional Asian Pacific American Caucus", and so on); it is applied to new and stored stories by the collector and to older feed files by the page, so a name is changed in one place. Broad Google News searches can return stories that only share a word with the communities (a Guam soccer score, a stock ticker), so a story found that way is shown only if its headline names the communities (`communityPattern`) or its publisher is trusted: every source in the list plus `trustedOutlets` (local Guam, Hawaii and Pacific outlets and U.S. community papers). Remove an outlet from `trustedOutlets` to hold it to the headline test as well. Stored stories are checked by the same rules on every update. Stories about Vietnamese Americans, Taiwanese Americans and the other communities named in `communityPattern` are never treated as news from abroad. linkedin.com and britannica.com are blocked as non-news.
- **Pew Research Center (Research tab):** six sources read pewresearch.org's own WordPress API (its topic feeds all return the same site-wide feed): the Asian Americans topic (reports and short reads, no filter), the Race & Ethnicity topic and two searches for "Pacific Islander", each kept only when the headline or summary names Asian American, Native Hawaiian or Pacific Islander communities (`filter` plus the source's own `match` pattern, which also knows "Asians in the U.S."). Report parts (Acknowledgments, Methodology, Appendix, numbered chapters) are skipped. Pew publishes a few such reports a year, so `"keepDays": 730` on those sources keeps their stories for two years instead of the 30-day default, and research stories are counted apart from the news cap (`researchMax`, 150). A source can set its own `keepDays` and `match`.
- **Pacific Islander coverage:** the "Google News: Pacific Islands" search looks for Pacific Islands, Pacific Islanders and Pasifika stories about climate, sea level, deep-sea mining, sovereignty, nuclear legacy, migration and the Compact of Free Association. `communityPattern` now also recognises "Pacific islands", Pasifika, Pacific peoples and communities, Polynesian, Melanesian, the Marshall Islands and the Cook Islands, so such a story passes the headline test. A site on a country domain such as independent.co.uk stays blocked.
- **Sources:** edit `sources.json`. `"filter": true` keeps only AAPI-relevant stories from general outlets. `"kind"` sets the type: `community` (shown as AAPI Media), `news` (Mainstream, the default for any source without a kind), `statement`, `research`. `"google": true` marks a Google News RSS search (personal, non-commercial use under Google's terms). `"fullText": true` saves article text for the reader (use it only for content you own or have rights to; the feed must include full post text). Set `"enabled": false` to switch a source off. `excludeHosts` lists sites that are never shown, with their subdomains (currently newsletters.theyappie.com, hoodline.com, vietnam.vn, dainikjagranmpcg.com, kelownadailycourier.ca, nationalenquirer.co and .com, sofascore.com, usresistnews.org, constitutioncenter.org, history.com and medium.com). Google News shows a publisher's name rather than its address, so each blocked site's name is also matched ("hoodline.com" blocks a publisher called Hoodline), and `excludeNames` adds names that differ from the address (for example The National Constitution Center). Stories already stored from a blocked site are removed on the next update.
- **Site name and icons:** the site is named "The Yappie Pro — Informing and Empowering AAPI Communities". That name is the tab title of every page, the `application-name` and `og:site_name` tags in the page head, and the `name` in `site.webmanifest`; a story's tab title is its headline followed by "The Yappie Pro". The icons in the site root (`favicon.ico` with 16, 32 and 48 px, `favicon-32.png`, `apple-touch-icon.png`, `icon-192.png`, `icon-512.png`) come from the Yappie "y" tile: the full tile for the large sizes and a tighter crop of the same art for the tab sizes, so the letter stays readable at 16 px. `policy.html`, `advocacy.html`, `research.html`, `saved.html` and `404.html` are copies of `index.html`, so a change to the head goes into all of them.
- **Which Yappie stories are listed:** every story on theyappie.com published on or after **23 September 2026**, except those in the **Press** or **Newsletters** categories (the newsletter issues live on newsletters.theyappie.com, which is blocked as a site as well). Three options on The Yappie's entry in `sources.json` do this, and any source can use them: `"excludeCategories": ["Press", "Newsletters"]` drops a story that has one of those categories in its feed, whatever its other categories are and however the name is capitalised (a copy stored by an earlier run is removed too); `"pages": 10` reads up to that many pages of a WordPress feed (`feed/?paged=2`, 10 stories a page) and stops at the first page that reaches back past the days the source's stories are kept, so the older stories come in on the first run and later runs only need page 1 in practice; `"keepSince": "2026-09-23"` keeps every story published on or after that day (UTC) however old it gets, instead of the usual 30 days (`keepDays`), and those stories do not count against the news cap (`maxItems`), so they never push other stories out. To change the day or the categories, edit those lines.
- **Yappie article page:** the byline author is a link to the author's page on theyappie.com (`/author/<name>/`). That address cannot be worked out from the name, so the collector reads it from the story's own page (the link whose text is the author's name), stores it as `byu` in `data/articles/<id>.json`, and reads each new author once (at most 6 pages per run). A byline whose stories live on another site, such as "The Yappie Staff" (newsletter stories), is listed under `"authors"` on The Yappie's entry in `sources.json`. An author with no page stays plain text. The article page shows no community chips.
- **Fonts:** the Yappie article viewer uses Corsario Variable for its headline and story text, and everything else (feed headlines, the details pane, chips) uses Tablet Gothic, both from an Adobe Fonts web project (the `<link>` near the top of `index.html`; the font files stay on Adobe's servers, because their terms do not allow self-hosting). IBM Plex Sans from Google Fonts is the backup if Adobe cannot be reached. To change fonts, edit that link and the `--head` (article viewer) and `--sans` (everything else) lines at the top of the page's CSS. Tablet Gothic is in the project as regular and bold only, so medium and semibold text shows as regular or bold; feed headlines and the grouped "More ... stories" headlines are set to regular.
- **Ways to read a source:** `"feed"` (RSS or Atom), `"wp"` (a WordPress REST list, for example `https://aapidata.com/wp-json/wp/v2/posts?categories=13&per_page=20&_fields=link,title,date_gmt,excerpt`), or `"page"` (an organization's own listing page; `"link"` is a regular expression that the path of each story link must match). A story found on a page needs a title and a date on that page, otherwise it is left out, and the page is read once per run even when two sources use it. Three optional settings help with pages that do not list dates plainly: `"embedded": {"title": …, "link": …, "date": …}` reads stories from JSON that the page carries in a `<script>` tag (used for the South China Morning Post topic page; dates may be ISO text or epoch seconds or milliseconds), `"idDate"` is a regular expression whose first group, found in the story link, is a 24-character story ID that begins with its creation time (used for HuffPost, which prints no dates), and `"attr"` keeps only links whose own attributes match a pattern (used to read HuffPost's main story list and not the ad module of other stories beside it). `"skipTitle"` on a news source drops results whose headline matches it, for pages that are not stories (see Nichi Bei below).
- **Added 31 Oct 2026 (live-checked once from a cloud sandbox):** AP-NORC (AAPI data topic feed, typed `research`, so it appears on the Research tab and not in News), Civil Beat (Native Hawaiian category feed) and Hawaii Public Radio (Native Hawaiian tag feed) are read from their feeds; The Juggernaut, Mochi Magazine, Angry Asian Man, Red, White and Brown (read through a Google News search of its Substack, because Substack answers GitHub's servers with HTTP 403; Nichi Bei is read the same way) and Pacific Citizen (WordPress REST, newest 20 posts) are AAPI Media sources; NextShark is read from its WordPress REST list because its /feed address now returns 404 (on 31 Oct 2026 it had published nothing in the previous 30 days, so it lists nothing until it posts again); the South China Morning Post is read from its topic page's embedded data; HuffPost's Asian Voices page is read as a page and dated from its story IDs. **The New York Times, AP News and Nichi Bei refuse automated page reads** (bot protection answered 403 or a challenge page), so they are read through Google News searches limited to their own site (`site:nytimes.com`, `site:apnews.com`, `site:nichibei.org`). The NYT and AP searches keep only headlines that name AAPI communities, so they approximate the topic pages and do not mirror them; Nichi Bei is a Japanese American community paper, so its search is unfiltered, except that author-name and archive index pages that Google returns as "headlines" are skipped. Links from these three go to the Google News redirect, which sends the reader to the publisher. NBC Asian America was already covered by the existing "NBC Asian America" entry (its listing page prints no dates, so it is read through Google News too). Stories about places abroad (for example India-only stories from The Juggernaut) are dropped by the "U.S. AAPI communities" rule; words such as Nisei, Sansei, Nikkei and JACL count as naming a U.S. community.
- **Statements** (`"kind": "statement"`, shown on the Advocacy tab, not in News) come from U.S. AAPI advocacy organizations, so a statement about a place abroad (for example deportations to Cambodia) is still listed. Sources, live-checked on 31 Oct 2026: TAAF, Stop AAPI Hate (press releases and commentary), Advancing Justice | AAJC, AAJA, CAPAC, OCA National, AALDEF, NCAPA, SEARAC, Sikh Coalition, SALDEF, NAPABA, NAPAWF, JACL, APIAVote, Asian American Scholar Forum and AAPI Data (press releases, blog and surveys). CAPAC's press releases are also in the Policy tracker as government statements. AALDEF and NAPABA block automated requests with a Cloudflare check, so they are read through a Google News search of their own pages (personal, non-commercial use under Google's terms).
- **Tagging rules:** all keyword rules live in one file, `taxonomy.json` (topics, communities, states, bill pattern, language check). The collector, the page and the WordPress plugin all read the same rules. After editing it, run `RETAG=1 node scripts/fetch.mjs` to re-tag stories already in the feed.
- **Policy sources:** `federalRegister` (rules and notices: `terms` find documents that name AAPI communities, `issueTerms` find open rules on related issues), `executiveActions` (presidential documents), `congress` (`days` to look back, `maxPages`), and the government feeds marked `"kind": "govstmt"` in `sources`. `policyKeepDays` (120) and `policyMax` (300) set how much is kept. To add a statement feed, add a source with `"kind": "govstmt"` and a `.gov` feed URL; add `"filter": true` for broad agency feeds so only AAPI-related items are kept.
- **Source list on the left rail (News tab):** The Yappie, AsAmNews, The Rebel Yellow and Joysauce are listed first; every other source is combined into one "Other sources" row that filters to all of them together. Edit `MAIN4` (the list returned by `MAINS()`) in `index.html` to change the four. The Research tab does the same with its own list in the same place: AAPI Data, Pew Research Center, AP-NORC and Associated Press, in that order, then one "Other sources" row. The Policy and Advocacy tabs list whatever sources are present. On the Policy tab the left-menu groups (Topics, Communities, Relevance, Sources) start with only Topics open; Policy remembers its own open/closed choices, separate from News.
- **Renaming or merging a source:** `"renames"` in `sources.json` maps an old source name to its new one (for example `"The Takeaway": "The Asian American Foundation"`, so the foundation's blog and press releases are one group, and `"Bloomberg.com": "Bloomberg News"` for a publisher Google News names by its address). Items already stored under the old name are renamed on the next run, and the page applies the same list to an older feed file. When you rename a source in the `sources` list, rename its entry in `kinds` too, because the kind is looked up by the new name.
- **Header:** one slim bar with the logo, the section tabs (News, Policy, Advocacy, Research, Saved), a compact search box that widens when you click into it (press `/` to jump to it), and the light/dark switch. Under 860px wide the tabs move to a second row.
- **Keys:** `/` search, `j`/`k` move, `Enter` open, `R` refresh, `Esc` reset.

## Things to know
- Topic, community, state and bill tags come from keyword matching on the headline and excerpt. They are fast and transparent but not perfect: a story can be tagged with a topic it only touches, and a story can miss a tag. The page has no About tab, so these limits are written down here.
- The English check is a word-list heuristic. It catches nearly all Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Vietnamese and other non-English headlines, and most Spanish ones, but a short Spanish headline can slip through.
- **Live check, 31 Oct 2026:** the collector was run once against the real Federal Register, Congress.gov, Regulations.gov, GovInfo, White House, Justice Department, USCIS and CAPAC servers from a cloud sandbox, and each answered in the expected format. USCIS sits behind a bot filter that answered one test request with 403, so it may be blocked from some networks. Three news-side feeds (NextShark, Asian Americans Advancing Justice, OCA) returned 404 and need new addresses. Nothing has been run from GitHub Actions itself. Fix or disable any source that shows FAIL in your first run log.
- **Boilerplate is filtered on purpose.** `sources.json` has `skipTitle` patterns that drop museum repatriation notices (they mention Native Hawaiian organizations but are not policy), paperwork-collection notices, ceremonial observances such as "Labor Day, 2026" when they are found only through an issue word, and individual court cases in USCIS and Justice Department releases unless they name the communities. Edit or remove a pattern if you want those items.
- **Titles decide for Regulations.gov and GovInfo.** Their list views carry no summary, so an item is kept only when its title names the communities or (for "Related issue") one of the issue words. Regulations.gov items that the Federal Register query already lists are not repeated.
- "Impacts AAPIs" and "Related issue" are keyword heuristics. Expect some records labeled "Related issue" that matter little, and some that matter but are missed. How records qualify: a rule or notice is kept if it names AAPI communities, or if it is a rule on a related issue whose comment period is still open. An executive action is kept if the Federal Register's search finds it through an AAPI term ("Impacts AAPIs") or an issue term ("Related issue"). A bill is "Impacts AAPIs" if its title or Congress.gov summary does, and "Related issue" if its title touches one of the issue words. Hearings and reports (kind "Hearings & reports") and public laws from GovInfo qualify the same way, on their titles.
- Not built: district-level filtering, a candidate tracker, hearing and court-docket feeds, and Spanish or other-language headlines.

## Etiquette
Link out, never republish full articles, and keep the User-Agent honest. If an outlet asks to be removed, disable it in `sources.json`.
